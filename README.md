# Fundamental-Processes-of-Nature-From-Elementary-Particles-to-Cosmic-Structures-and-Biological-Life-
Author: Sanjay Soni Research Identity: Sanjay Soni Research Research Area: Conceptual Science and Philosophy Framework: Action-Flow Theory — A Process-Based Conceptual Framework Research Status: Conceptual hypothesis open to scientific review, mathematical analysis, experimental testing, and independent verification.
. Abstract

Nature contains structures at many levels, ranging from elementary particles and atomic nuclei to atoms, molecules, stars, planets, and living organisms. Each level exhibits physical interactions, transformations, and characteristic forms of organization.

Modern science investigates these phenomena through different theories and disciplines, including particle physics, nuclear physics, electromagnetism, chemistry, astronomy, and biology.

This conceptual study asks whether the formation, activity, and transformation of structures at different levels can be examined within a shared process-based framework.

The central idea is that physical interactions and transformations contribute to the formation of new states and organized structures. These structures can subsequently participate in further interactions, producing additional transformations and outcomes.

The proposed framework does not claim that all natural processes have one proven mechanism. Instead, it presents an open research question concerning the relationships between physical interactions, structural organization, and biological activity.

2. Fundamental Scientific Background

2.1 Quarks and Gluons

Quarks are elementary particles that participate in the strong interaction. Gluons mediate the strong interaction between quarks within quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

Protons and neutrons are composite particles. A proton's two up quarks and one down quark have a combined electric charge of +1 elementary charge. A neutron's one up quark and two down quarks have a combined electric charge of zero.

The gluon is not an electric charge. It is the carrier of the strong interaction and itself participates in the strong interaction through its color charge.

These established properties provide a starting point for examining how fundamental interactions contribute to the formation and behavior of matter.

2.2 Atomic Structure and Electromagnetic Interactions

Atomic nuclei contain protons and, in most cases, neutrons. Electrons occupy quantum states associated with the nucleus.

Electromagnetic interactions are essential to atomic structure, chemical bonding, and many interactions between charged particles. The energy of an atom depends on its physical state and interactions with its surroundings.

Energy is a physical quantity, whereas electric charge is a property of particles and fields. They are related through physical laws but are not identical.

This distinction is essential when developing a scientifically consistent process-based model.

2.3 Chemical Organization

Atoms can combine through chemical interactions to form molecules and more complex structures.

Chemical reactions involve changes in molecular composition, bonding, and energy. Under suitable conditions, complex chemical systems can develop organized behavior.

Chemistry therefore provides an important link between atomic interactions and the structures required for biological processes.

2.4 Biological Organization

Living organisms depend on coordinated physical and chemical processes.

Molecules participate in biochemical reactions, cells maintain organized internal processes, and tissues and organs perform specialized functions through interactions among many components.

Biological activity is not explained by atomic structure alone. It also depends on molecular organization, cellular regulation, environmental conditions, and evolutionary history.

Nevertheless, biological systems operate through physical and chemical processes rather than independently of them.

3. From Fundamental Interactions to Complex Structures

The following sequence presents a simplified conceptual map:

Elementary particles → Nuclear structures → Atoms → Molecules → Chemical networks → Cells → Tissues and organs → Organisms

At cosmic scales, another set of processes contributes to the formation of large-scale structures:

Matter and radiation → Gravitational evolution → Stars and galaxies → Stellar element formation → Planetary systems

These sequences are related, but they are not one simple, universal linear chain. Different interactions dominate at different scales, and several processes occur simultaneously.

The framework proposes investigating how interactions, changes of state, and structural organization connect these different levels.

4. The Central Conceptual Hypothesis

The central hypothesis of this study is:

Natural structures emerge, persist, and transform through physical interactions and processes operating under particular conditions. The resulting structures can participate in further interactions, producing new states and forms of organization.

This hypothesis can be examined at several levels:

1. Fundamental particles and their interactions.
2. The formation and properties of atomic nuclei.
3. Atomic structure and electromagnetic interactions.
4. Chemical bonding and molecular organization.
5. Biochemical reactions and cellular activity.
6. The formation and evolution of cosmic structures.
7. The development, maintenance, and eventual breakdown of living organisms.

The framework seeks to identify common process patterns without assuming that the underlying mechanisms are identical.

5. Action-Flow Theory: A Conceptual Perspective

Within my proposed Action-Flow Theory, I examine nature through the relationship between interaction, change, and outcome.

A preliminary conceptual sequence is:

Interaction → Change → New State → Further Interaction → New Outcome

This sequence is intended as a general way to organize questions about natural processes. It is not a replacement for established physical equations or domain-specific scientific explanations.

The research question is whether this framework can help compare different processes while preserving the differences between nuclear, electromagnetic, chemical, biological, and gravitational mechanisms.

The term action-flow is used here as a conceptual label, not as a claim that all physical phenomena share one experimentally established mechanism.

6. Structure, Activity, and the Concept of an Actor

A further philosophical question concerns the relationship between a structure and its activity.

Atoms participate in physical interactions. Molecules participate in chemical reactions. Cells carry out regulated biological functions. Organisms exhibit coordinated activities through interconnected biological systems.

These examples raise a question:

Can the relationship between a system, its interactions, and its resulting activity be described consistently across different levels of organization?

In this framework, the term actor is a conceptual term for a system or organized structure participating in a process. Its use does not establish the existence of a separate physical entity beyond the system itself.

Whether this terminology offers scientific explanatory value must be assessed through clear definitions and comparison with established models.

7. Research Questions

This study identifies the following questions for further investigation:

1. Which process patterns are genuinely shared across different levels of physical organization?
2. How do fundamental interactions contribute to the formation and stability of different structures?
3. How can electromagnetic interactions be related quantitatively to atomic and molecular behavior?
4. How does chemical organization contribute to biological activity?
5. Which aspects of biological organization require explanations beyond the properties of individual atoms and molecules?
6. Can a common process-based framework describe these relationships without ignoring the specific laws operating at each level?
7. Can the proposed framework generate testable predictions that distinguish it from existing scientific explanations?

These questions are intended to encourage investigation rather than imply that their answers are already known.

8. Scientific Limitations

This work is a conceptual framework, not a completed physical theory.

It does not establish that electric charge is the origin of all energy, that electromagnetic interactions explain every form of motion, or that nuclear, chemical, and biological processes arise from one identical mechanism.

The strong interaction, electromagnetic interaction, weak interaction, and gravitational interaction have distinct physical descriptions. Their relationships must be addressed using the appropriate scientific theories.

Likewise, the origin of life, biological evolution, and the formation of cosmic structures involve different scientific questions and bodies of evidence.

Any proposed unification would require precise definitions, mathematical formulation, consistency with established observations, and independently reproducible tests.

9. Proposed Research Method

Further development of this framework may proceed through the following steps:

Step 1 — Define terms: Establish precise meanings for interaction, process, structure, state, change, and outcome.

Step 2 — Review established science: Identify what current theories already explain at each level.

Step 3 — Compare processes: Examine similarities and differences between nuclear, atomic, chemical, biological, and cosmic processes.

Step 4 — Develop a mathematical description: Express any proposed common relationships using quantities that can be defined and measured.

Step 5 — Generate predictions: Identify outcomes that could be tested against observations or experiments.

Step 6 — Independent review: Invite researchers to evaluate the framework, identify errors, and suggest improvements.

Step 7 — Revise or reject unsupported claims: Modify the framework wherever evidence contradicts its assumptions.

10. Conclusion

The universe contains structures that form, interact, change, and participate in further processes. From elementary particles and atomic nuclei to chemical systems and living organisms, physical interactions play essential roles in the behavior and organization of matter.

Modern science explains these phenomena through several interconnected but distinct disciplines.

My proposed research direction is to investigate whether a shared process-based framework can help describe relationships between interactions, structural formation, activity, and transformation across different levels of nature.

The purpose is not to declare a new universal law without evidence. It is to present a conceptual question that can be clarified, mathematically examined, tested, criticized, and improved through scientific collaboration.

Experience is an idea, not a claim. Scientific conclusions require evidence, testing, and independent verification.
